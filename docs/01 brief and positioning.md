## The dual positioning (this is the key idea)

The site carries two audiences without feeling split. Use a **shared visual identity**
but let the copy and CTAs flex:

| | Personal (recruiter) | Agency — "Dev Logic Max" (client) |
|---|---|---|
| Wants to know | Seniority, real systems, reliability, leadership | Can you ship *my* product, on time, maintainable |
| Proof | Production systems, architecture depth, CI/CD, scale | Case studies, outcomes, process, testimonials |
| CTA | "See my work" / "Download CV" / "Let's talk" | "Start a project" / "Book a call" |

## Brand voice

- **Confident, plain, specific.** Show numbers and systems, don't use hype adjectives.
  "Multi-tenant HR platform serving Italian clients in production" beats "amazing scalable app."
- **Senior, not salesy.** Let the work carry the weight. One crisp value line, then proof.
- **Global-professional English**, lightly warm. No jargon walls, no buzzword soup.
- Every button says what happens: "Download CV," "View case study," "Email Abdul."

## What to emphasize (ranked)

1. **Production SaaS + multi-tenant architecture** — the strongest differentiator.
2. **DevOps ownership** (VPS, Docker, CI/CD, Nginx) — shows he ships *and* runs.
3. **Integration/compliance depth** (ATS, webhooks, GDPR, RBAC).
4. **Breadth of stack** (front + back + infra).
5. **Agency capability** (Dev Logic Max as a studio, not just a name).

## What to avoid

- Toy projects presented at the same weight as real products.
- Generic "passionate developer who loves coding" copy.
- Claiming skills without a visible artifact (repo, live site, case study).
- Over-promising agency scale — position it as a sharp, senior-led studio, honestly.
